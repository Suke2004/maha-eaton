Q1 Quantitative Aptitude • Time & Work (Automated Test Fixtures)
Medium
At the Eaton India Innovation Centre (EIIC) testing lab, two automated test fixtures, Rig A and Rig B, are used to perform endurance testing on a batch of Bussmann fast-acting fuses. Rig A alone can test the entire batch in 12 hours, while Rig B alone takes 18 hours. If the test engineer starts Rig A at 8:00 AM and alternates between Rig A and Rig B every 1 hour (Rig A for hour 1, Rig B for hour 2, Rig A for hour 3, and so on), at what time will the batch testing be completely finished?
A) 8:12 PM (12 hours 12 minutes)
B) 10:20 PM (14 hours 20 minutes)
C) 10:30 PM (14 hours 30 minutes)
D) 11:00 PM (15 hours 0 minutes)
B) 10:20 PM (14 hours 20 minutes)
Step-by-step mathematical solution:
1. Define Total Work: Let the total testing workload be the LCM of individual completion times: LCM(12, 18) = 36 units of work.
2. Determine Hourly Work Rates:
• Work done by Rig A in 1 hour = 36 / 12 = 3 units/hour.
• Work done by Rig B in 1 hour = 36 / 18 = 2 units/hour.
3. Calculate Work in 1 Alternating Cycle (2 Hours):
• In the first 2 hours (Rig A for 1 hr + Rig B for 1 hr), work completed = 3 + 2 = 5 units.
4. Determine Complete Cycles:
• Number of complete 2-hour cycles to get close to 36 units: 36 / 5 = 7 full cycles (remainder = 1 unit).
• Time for 7 cycles = 7 × 2 hours = 14 hours.
• Work completed after 14 hours = 7 × 5 = 35 units.
5. Remaining Work & Next Turn:
• Remaining work = 36 - 35 = 1 unit.
• After 7 full cycles, it is Rig A's turn again.
• Time taken by Rig A to complete 1 unit = 1 unit / (3 units/hr) = 1/3 hour = 20 minutes.
6. Total Elapsed Time:
• Total time = 14 hours + 20 minutes = 14 hours 20 minutes.
• Start time = 8:00 AM → Adding 14 hours gives 10:00 PM; adding 20 minutes gives 10:20 PM.
🧠 The Main Concept Behind This Answer
Core Principle: The Unit Work & Cycle Method in Alternating Rate Problems
• The LCM Convention: Never calculate with fractional fractions like 1/12 + 1/18 if you want speed and zero arithmetic errors in online assessments. Always set Total Work = LCM of times.
• The Cycle Concept: In alternating work problems, determine the net work done per full period (here, a 2-hour window). Multiply by the integer quotient to reach the largest multiple less than the total work.
• The Boundary Trap: Online test makers intentionally set questions where the final fraction of work is completed by the first worker before the second worker's turn arrives. Always track exactly whose turn it is for the remaining workload.
• Eaton Context: Test automation scheduling, sustaining cycle-time benchmarking, and multi-channel hardware testing require understanding parallel and interleaved resource utilization.
Q2 Quantitative Aptitude • Speed, Time & Distance / Signal Propagation
Medium
A telecommunications engineer at Eaton is using Time Domain Reflectometry (TDR) to locate an impedance mismatch fault in a high-speed shielded transmission line. The electromagnetic test pulse propagates through the dielectric cable at a velocity of 2 × 108 m/s. The TDR instrument transmits an incident pulse and detects the reflected echo pulse exactly 1.4 microseconds (μs) later. What is the precise distance from the instrument to the fault location?
A) 280 meters
B) 140 meters
C) 70 meters
D) 210 meters
B) 140 meters
Step-by-step mathematical solution:
1. Identify Given Parameters:
• Propagation speed in cable, v = 2 × 108 m/s.
• Total round-trip elapsed time, Δt = 1.4 μs = 1.4 × 10-6 s.
2. Understand Round-Trip Physics:
• The pulse travels from the transmitter to the fault location (distance d) and then reflects back from the fault to the detector (another distance d).
• Total distance traveled by the pulse = 2 × d.
3. Apply Distance Equation:
• 2d = v × Δt
• d = (v × Δt) / 2
• d = (2 × 108 m/s × 1.4 × 10-6 s) / 2
• d = (2.8 × 102 m) / 2 = 280 / 2 = 140 meters.
🧠 The Main Concept Behind This Answer
Core Principle: Two-Way Time-of-Flight (ToF) & Time Domain Reflectometry (TDR)
• The Factor of 2: In all echo-ranging technologies (TDR, Radar, Sonar, Ultrasound, Lidar), the measured time Δt represents round-trip transit. Forgetting to divide by 2 is the single most common trap in engineering aptitude tests.
• Propagation Velocity in Cables: Signals in copper coax or twisted-pair do not travel at the vacuum speed of light (c = 3 × 108 m/s); they travel at v = c / √εr, where εr is the relative dielectric permittivity of the insulation (typically PVC or PTFE, giving v ≈ 0.66c ≈ 2 × 108 m/s).
• Eaton Application: TDR is standard in sustaining engineering to inspect harness integrity, fuse link continuity in sealed packages, and PCB trace discontinuities.
Q3 Quantitative Aptitude • Percentages & Tolerance Compounding
Easy-Medium
A high-precision current-sensing shunt resistor used in a Bussmann smart fuse monitor has a nominal resistance of 50.0 mΩ. Under elevated operating temperature during continuous rated current testing, its resistance initially drifts upward by +4.0%. Subsequently, accelerated thermal aging in the environmental chamber causes an additional +2.5% increase on the drifted value. What is the net percentage increase over nominal, and what is the final measured resistance?
A) +6.50% net increase; 53.25 mΩ
B) +6.60% net increase; 53.30 mΩ
C) +6.75% net increase; 53.38 mΩ
D) +6.40% net increase; 53.20 mΩ
B) +6.60% net increase; 53.30 mΩ
Step-by-step mathematical solution:
1. Net Successive Percentage Formula:
• For two successive percentage changes x% and y%, the net effective percentage change is:
    Net % = x + y + (x × y) / 100
• Here x = +4.0%, y = +2.5%
• Net % = 4.0 + 2.5 + (4.0 × 2.5) / 100 = 6.5 + (10.0 / 100) = 6.5 + 0.10 = +6.60%.
2. Calculate Final Resistance:
• Rfinal = Rnominal × (1 + 0.066)
• Rfinal = 50.0 mΩ × 1.066 = 53.30 mΩ.
3. Alternative Stepwise Check:
• After 1st drift: 50.0 × 1.04 = 52.0 mΩ.
• After 2nd drift: 52.0 × 1.025 = 52.0 + 1.30 = 53.30 mΩ.
• Net increase = (53.30 - 50.0)/50.0 = 3.30 / 50.0 = 6.60%.
🧠 The Main Concept Behind This Answer
Core Principle: Successive Compounding vs. Simple Linear Addition
• The Additive Mistake: Most students mistakenly add 4% + 2.5% = 6.5%. But subsequent physical degradation acts on the already expanded baseline, creating a compounding term (xy/100).
• TCR (Temperature Coefficient of Resistance): In precision instrumentation and Bussmann electronics, resistors drift according to R(T) = R0[1 + α(T - T0)]. When thermal drift combines with long-term aging/metallurgical oxidation, cascading multipliers must be evaluated to ensure sensing accuracy stays within ADC calibration limits.

Q5 Quantitative Aptitude • Combinatorics & Probability / QC Lot Sampling
Medium-Hard
A production lot of 20 specialized Eaton Bussmann surface-mount fuses contains exactly 16 compliant units and 4 defective units with out-of-spec clearing times. A quality assurance engineer selects a random sample of 3 fuses simultaneously without replacement for destructive test verification. What is the probability that the sample contains at least one defective fuse?
A) 28/57 (≈ 49.1%)
B) 29/57 (≈ 50.9%)
C) 1/5 (≈ 20.0%)
D) 7/19 (≈ 36.8%)
B) 29/57 (≈ 50.9%)
Step-by-step mathematical solution:
1. Use the Complementary Probability Strategy:
• P(at least 1 defective) = 1 - P(exactly 0 defective) = 1 - P(all 3 fuses are compliant).
2. Calculate Total Possible Combinations:
• Number of ways to choose 3 fuses out of 20 = 20C3
• 20C3 = (20 × 19 × 18) / (3 × 2 × 1) = 20 × 19 × 3 = 1,140 total outcomes.
3. Calculate Favorable Ways to Choose 3 Compliant Fuses:
• All 3 must come from the 16 compliant fuses = 16C3
• 16C3 = (16 × 15 × 14) / (3 × 2 × 1) = 16 × 5 × 7 = 560 ways.
4. Compute P(All 3 Compliant):
• P(All compliant) = 560 / 1140 = 56 / 114 = 28 / 57 (≈ 0.4912).
5. Compute Complementary Probability:
• P(at least 1 defective) = 1 - (28 / 57) = (57 - 28) / 57 = 29 / 57 (≈ 0.5088 or 50.9%).
🧠 The Main Concept Behind This Answer
Core Principle: The Complement Rule in Quality Reliability Sampling
• The Power of P(at least one) = 1 - P(none): Calculating P(≥1) directly requires evaluating P(1 defective) + P(2 defective) + P(3 defective), which triples calculation time and error opportunities. Always use the complement rule.
• Hypergeometric Sampling without Replacement: When sample size is significant relative to lot size (3 out of 20 = 15%), consecutive draws alter the denominator (16/20 × 15/19 × 14/18 = 28/57). Do not use independent binomial approximation when sampling without replacement from small populations.
• Eaton Alignment: Acceptance Quality Limit (AQL) destructive testing is a core activity in fusible product sustaining engineering.

Q7 Quantitative Aptitude • System Reliability & MTBF (Series Architecture)
Hard
A smart Eaton fusible disconnect switch comprises three critical electronic sub-assemblies connected in a series reliability configuration (any sub-assembly failure causes the whole unit to fail):
• Microcontroller Supervisory Board: MTBF = 100,000 hours
• Isolated DC-DC Auxiliary Supply: MTBF = 50,000 hours
• Electronic Trip Solenoid Actuator: MTBF = 200,000 hours

Assuming constant failure rates (exponential distribution), what is the overall system failure rate (λsys) in failures per hour, the system MTBF, and the probability that the disconnect switch operates without failure for a mission time of 10,000 operating hours?
A) λ = 3.5 × 10-5 /hr; MTBF = 28,571 hrs; R(10,000) ≈ 70.5%
B) λ = 1.0 × 10-5 /hr; MTBF = 100,000 hrs; R(10,000) ≈ 90.5%
C) λ = 7.0 × 10-5 /hr; MTBF = 14,286 hrs; R(10,000) ≈ 49.7%
D) λ = 2.0 × 10-5 /hr; MTBF = 50,000 hrs; R(10,000) ≈ 81.9%
A) λ = 3.5 × 10^-5 /hr; MTBF = 28,571 hrs; R(10,000) ≈ 70.5%
Step-by-step reliability mathematics:
1. Calculate Individual Failure Rates (λ = 1 / MTBF):
• λMCU = 1 / 100,000 = 1.0 × 10-5 failures/hr.
• λSupply = 1 / 50,000 = 2.0 × 10-5 failures/hr.
• λActuator = 1 / 200,000 = 0.5 × 10-5 failures/hr.
2. Calculate Total System Failure Rate for Series Configuration:
• In a series reliability model, failure rates add directly:
    λsys = λ1 + λ2 + λ3
• λsys = (1.0 + 2.0 + 0.5) × 10-5 = 3.5 × 10-5 failures/hour (or 35,000 FIT, where 1 FIT = 1 failure / 109 hrs).
3. Calculate Overall System MTBF:
• MTBFsys = 1 / λsys = 1 / (3.5 × 10-5) = 100,000 / 3.5 = 28,571.4 hours.
• Notice that system MTBF is lower than the lowest individual component MTBF (50,000 hrs)!
4. Calculate Reliability R(t) at t = 10,000 hours:
• For exponential distribution: R(t) = e-λsys · t.
• λsys · t = (3.5 × 10-5) × 10,000 = 0.35.
• R(10,000) = e-0.35 ≈ 0.7047 (70.5%).
5. Conclusion: Matches Option A.
🧠 The Main Concept Behind This Answer
Core Principle: Series System Reliability & The Exponential Failure Law
• The Series Reliability Trap: Students often mistakenly average MTBFs. But a series system is only as reliable as its weakest link; failure rates add, which forces overall MTBF to be strictly less than the smallest individual MTBF.
• Eaton Sustaining Engineering Context: Electronic disconnect switches and smart fuse monitors in industrial substations undergo rigorous FIT/MTBF calculations to verify compliance with safety integrity levels (SIL).
Q8 Quantitative Aptitude • Rule of Alligation (Fusible Solder Metallurgy)
Medium
In the manufacturing of specialized low-temperature Bussmann thermal cutoff links, metallurgical engineers prepare an alloy containing exactly 58% Bismuth (Bi) by weight by blending two available precursor tin-bismuth (Sn-Bi) alloys:
• Alloy A contains 40% Bismuth by weight.
• Alloy B contains 70% Bismuth by weight.

If the engineering batch starts with exactly 30 kg of Alloy A, how many kilograms of Alloy B must be blended with it to achieve the exact 58% target composition?
A) 36 kg
B) 42 kg
C) 45 kg
D) 50 kg
C) 45 kg
Step-by-step alligation & ratio solution:
1. Set Up the Alligation Cross:
• Percentage of Alloy A = 40%
• Percentage of Alloy B = 70%
• Target Mean Percentage = 58%
2. Compute Numerical Differences:
• Difference across Alloy B: |70 - 58| = 12 parts of Alloy A.
• Difference across Alloy A: |58 - 40| = 18 parts of Alloy B.
3. Find Mass Ratio of Alloy A to Alloy B:
• Ratio (Mass A / Mass B) = 12 / 18 = 2 / 3.
4. Calculate Required Mass of Alloy B:
• Given Mass of Alloy A = 30 kg.
• (30 kg) / (Mass B) = 2 / 3
• Mass B = 30 × (3 / 2) = 15 × 3 = 45 kg.
5. Verification:
• Total mass = 30 + 45 = 75 kg.
• Bi from A = 30 × 0.40 = 12 kg.
• Bi from B = 45 × 0.70 = 31.5 kg.
• Total Bi = 12 + 31.5 = 43.5 kg.
• Net Bi % = (43.5 / 75) × 100 = 58.0%. Exact match!
🧠 The Main Concept Behind This Answer
Core Principle: The Alligation Cross for Binary Mixtures
• In metallurgical and chemical formulation problems, the Alligation Method is 4x faster than setting up simultaneous linear algebraic equations.
• Fusible Solder Physics: Thermal fuses rely on precise eutectic compositions (such as 58% Bi / 42% Sn, melting sharply at 138°C) where liquidus and solidus temperatures coincide with zero plastic range.

Q14 Logical & Analytical Reasoning • Data Sufficiency in Logic Circuits

Statement (2): Intermediate output W is HIGH (1).
A) Statement (1) ALONE is sufficient, but Statement (2) alone is not sufficient.
B) Statement (2) ALONE is sufficient, but Statement (1) alone is not sufficient.
C) BOTH Statements (1) and (2) TOGETHER are sufficient, but NEITHER alone is sufficient.
D) Statements (1) and (2) TOGETHER are NOT sufficient.
A) Statement (1) ALONE is sufficient, but Statement (2) alone is not sufficient.
Step-by-step Data Sufficiency analysis:
1. Formulate the Boolean Expression:
• Intermediate node: W = X ⊕ Y.
• Final output: OUT = W · Z = (X ⊕ Y) · Z.
• OUT is 1 if and only if both (X ⊕ Y) = 1 AND Z = 1.
2. Evaluate Statement (1): 'Z is HIGH (1), and X ≠ Y':
• By definition of an XOR gate, when inputs are unequal (X ≠ Y, i.e., X=1,Y=0 or X=0,Y=1), the output W = X ⊕ Y = 1.
• Statement 1 also specifies that Z = 1.
• Therefore, OUT = 1 · 1 = 1.
• We get a definite, unique 'YES'. Statement (1) alone is SUFFICIENT.
3. Evaluate Statement (2): 'Intermediate output W is HIGH (1)':
• Knowing W = 1 tells us OUT = 1 · Z = Z.
• However, Statement (2) provides NO information about the state of input Z.
• If Z = 1, OUT = 1. If Z = 0, OUT = 0.
• We cannot determine a unique answer. Statement (2) alone is NOT SUFFICIENT.
4. Conclusion: Statement (1) ALONE is sufficient, but Statement (2) alone is not sufficient.

• The Goal of Data Sufficiency: You are not asked to compute a numerical answer; you must evaluate whether the information provided guarantees a single, definitive truth value (Yes or No).

Failure analysis and sustaining engineering methodology:
1. Physical Root Cause (CTE Mismatch):
• Alumina ceramic body: Coefficient of Thermal Expansion ≈ 6 to 7 ppm/°C.
• FR4 PCB with heavy copper planes: CTE ≈ 16 to 18 ppm/°C.
• During temperature swings (ΔT = 165 °C), the PCB expands and contracts nearly 3 times more than the ceramic fuse. This differential expansion creates intense cyclic mechanical shear stress concentrated entirely in the solder joint interfaces, causing creep-fatigue micro-voids and cracking (Coffin-Manson fatigue law).
2. Engineering Remedies:
• Modify footprint pads to create larger solder fillet volume that absorbs strain.
• Introduce J-leads, gull-wing leads, or flexible end-caps that mechanically flex under expansion.
• Evaluate high-reliability solder alloys with antimony/silver dopants engineered for automotive thermal cycling.
3. JD Alignment: This directly tests the candidate's understanding of product testing, teardown failure analysis, and sustaining engineering responsibilities.
🧠 The Main Concept Behind This Answer
Core Principle: Thermomechanical Stress & Coffin-Manson Solder Fatigue
• In automotive and industrial electronics, thermal shock and temperature cycling are the #1 cause of field solder joint failures.
• Never alter the test criteria to mask failures; identify the underlying material science mismatch and implement robust mechanical design mitigations.
